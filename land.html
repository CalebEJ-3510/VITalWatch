Clinical-trial oversight · pharmacovigilance

See the risk. Follow the evidence. Act with confidence.

VITalWatch brings clinical-trial oversight and pharmacovigilance into one auditable workspace — adverse-event intake with statutory reporting clocks, safety-signal detection, an escalation workflow that pauses a trial until a structured response is reviewed, and an audit trail that can prove it has not been altered.

01 — Why it exists

Important events should not disappear inside routine activity.

Trial data, safety cases, evidence and compliance history usually live in different places. A serious event can sit unremarked in a queue of routine ones; a statistical signal means nothing until a human reviews it in context. Teams need to know what changed, why it matters, who owns it, and what action is due — and to prove the answer later.

02 — What it is

Five connected pillars, one record of the truth.

Each pillar is built and demonstrable in the demo today. Each is honest about where it stops — the boundary is part of the feature, not a footnote.

Safety intake & reporting clocks

Adverse events are coded as the narrative arrives, and the statutory reporting clock starts the moment an event is marked serious — under the New Drugs & Clinical Trials Rules, 2019 framing.

Signal detection & escalation

Coded terms are screened by proportional reporting ratio — a triage order for human review, never a verdict. A flagged term can be raised straight into a safety-concern escalation, with the PRR finding attached for the reviewer.

Escalation & decision workflow

An escalation carries the raised facts, the company's nine-field structured response, the reviewer's resolve-or-escalate decision, and leadership's reasoned final decision — each step distinct, named and timestamped.

03 — How it works

One workflow, from detection to proof.

Every stage states its input, what the system derives, the decision a human must take, the evidence retained — and what the system does not claim.

01 Detect 02 Understand 03 Investigate 04 Decide 05 Prove
  1. Stage 01Detect
    Input
    Site-reported adverse-event narratives, visit and enrolment data as they arrive.
    System derives
    Coded terms, statutory reporting clocks started at intake, PRR screening across coded terms, alerts ranked by severity.
    Human decides
    Whether an event is serious; whether a flagged pattern merits review.
    Evidence kept
    The original narrative, the coded term, the intake timestamp, the clock state.
    Not claimed
    A flagged ratio is a triage order, not a safety finding.
  2. Stage 02Understand
    Input
    A flagged term, a deadline, a metric that moved.
    System derives
    Context in one place — the cases behind a ratio, the trial behind a deadline, enrolment against plan-to-date, and the freshness of every value.
    Human decides
    Whether context explains the pattern, or the pattern survives context.
    Evidence kept
    The values as of the moment they were read — every figure carries its as-of instant.
    Not claimed
    Incidence and causation. Sub-threshold rows stay visible so the criterion can be seen doing its work.
  3. Stage 03Investigate
    Input
    A pattern worth a human's time — raised as a protocol-deviation or safety-concern escalation.
    System derives
    The escalation package: the raised facts, the trial's paused state, the statutory response clock, and — for safety concerns — the AI signal finding with its engine version, confidence and review state.
    Human decides
    The company responds in nine mandatory structured fields; the raising role decides whether that response resolves the concern or goes to leadership.
    Evidence kept
    Every transition — actor, role, timestamp, and the full text of each structured field, never truncated.
    Not claimed
    An AI finding is not a medical conclusion; it stays unreviewed until a named human disposes of it.
  4. Stage 04Decide
    Input
    The assembled package, escalated to leadership when the response does not resolve it.
    System derives
    A structured decision record — continue, pause, resume, request further review, or terminate — with a mandatory reason and individually tracked conditions.
    Human decides
    The decision. Always. The system never decides, never auto-closes.
    Evidence kept
    Before and after state for every material change.
    Not claimed
    That an external authority received anything — this system records no external submission receipts.
  5. Stage 05Prove
    Input
    Any material change.
    System derives
    An append-only audit row — actor, role, action, reason, timestamp — hash-linked to the row before it. Verification names the first altered row.
    Human decides
    When to verify.
    Evidence kept
    Everything. UPDATE and DELETE are refused by the database itself.
    Not claimed
    The chain proves entries were not altered after the fact — it cannot prove they were honest when written.
04 — Who it helps

Built for the people who carry the trial.

Five roles sign in to the demo — one account per role; the selector below previews what each one is for. It changes this page's content only — permissions are granted by the signed-in account and enforced by the server, never by a control on a public page.

“What is the state of my participation?”

Sees first

Their own trial, upcoming appointments, consent status and history, and the adverse-event self-report form — nothing else.

Value

Registration, consent and reporting in one place, with the currently published consent form shown before any decision is asked for.

Boundary

Strictly scoped to their own participant record — a volunteer cannot see another participant's row, and the schema has no name or date-of-birth field anywhere to see.

“What does my trial need from me right now?”

Sees first

Pending escalations awaiting the company's structured response, the full escalation history, and the operations screen for eligibility criteria, forms and appointments.

Value

The response is nine mandatory fields — explanation through responsible person — so a corrective-action plan arrives complete, not assembled over email.

Boundary

The company responds; it never reviews its own response and never decides the outcome — that belongs to the raising role and to leadership.

“Which submissions and escalations need my decision today?”

Sees first

Registration submissions awaiting an eligibility decision and protocol-deviation escalations awaiting review, with the AI pre-screen finding beside each submission.

Value

The AI pre-screen is visible with its confidence — but the eligibility decision is the investigator's, and the record names them.

Boundary

Trial access is selected from the trial list, not assigned — the demo has no stored “my trials” ownership, and the interface says so rather than implying it.

“Which event or reporting obligation requires attention next?”

Sees first

Uncoded adverse events, PRR-ranked signals and safety-concern escalations, with the 24-hour / 14-day statutory clocks derived live at read time.

Value

Coding assistance suggests a term with its match basis; the officer confirms, corrects, or leaves it uncoded — the suggestion never codes the record by itself.

Boundary

Clocks are computed from intake. The system does not record that an external authority received a filing.

“Is the institution safe, compliant, and inspection-ready?”

Sees first

Escalations under leadership review, portfolio KPIs, and live audit-chain verification — with global search and the audit trail reserved to this role.

Value

Final decisions carry a mandatory reason and individually tracked conditions; verification walks the chain and names the first altered row.

Boundary

“Chain verified” certifies the integrity of stored records — not the organization, the deployment, or regulatory compliance. Leadership oversees everything and conducts nothing.

05 — Trust & safety

Trust is engineered, not asserted.

Evidence is visible

Every claim on screen links to the record it came from. Raised facts, AI-generated findings and human decisions are visually distinct — always.

Patterns stop at human review

Detection produces a triage order. A human reviews, a human decides, and the decision record names them.

Uncertainty is shown

Missing or unevaluable data renders as unknown — dotted, labelled, never silently zero. Conflicting and incomplete states are designed, not improvised.

Freshness is stated

Deadlines and metrics carry their as-of instant. Stale data is marked stale and shows when it was last known good.

Changes keep their history

Material mutations retain before and after state. Nothing material is silently overwritten — the audit row carries actor, role, action, reason and timestamp.

Data is minimized

The demonstration schema has no name, no date of birth and no resolvable identifier to put real participant data into. Pseudonymous codes are the records.

06 — Proof points

What is true — and what we do not claim.

  • Synthetic data, clearly labelled. Every record is generated; the dataset is anchored to a fixed snapshot (2026-08-22) and regenerates identically from a fixed seed.
  • Material mutations are audited. Writes carry actor, role, reason and before/after state into an append-only chain you can verify yourself in the demo.
  • Pattern detection stops at human review. PRR screening produces a triage order with its criterion visible; no automated conclusion is ever recorded.
  • AI findings stay human-gated. Eligibility pre-screens, coding suggestions and signal findings persist with engine version, input summary and confidence — and stay unreviewed until a named human disposes of them.
  • Database boundaries are defined. The same application runs on SQLite for the demo and Postgres for deployment, with an identical audit-chain head hash on both.
  • No customer logos, no patient outcomes. There are none to show, and none are invented. This page carries no testimonials and no adoption claims.
  • No regulatory approvals or compliance certifications. Nothing here is certified, validated, or approved for clinical use by any authority.
  • No licensed dictionaries. MedDRA and WHODrug are commercial and are not used; coding runs against a curated vocabulary written for this demonstration.
  • No live external integrations — and no export surface. No EDC feed, no ABDM exchange, no FHIR/SDTM export, no external submission receipts — each is a stated boundary, not a hidden gap.
07 — About

A demonstration of what safety-critical software should feel like.

VITalWatch is a demonstration system for clinical-trial oversight and pharmacovigilance, framed on the AYUSH clinical-research context — the regulatory vocabulary of the New Drugs & Clinical Trials Rules 2019, CTRI registration, the DPDP Act 2023, and ALCOA+ data-integrity principles. It is built for the people who evaluate, study and design safety-critical workflows: clinical operations teams, pharmacovigilance officers, researchers and reviewers.

The design goal is operational clarity in safety-critical work: no value without its reference line, no deadline without its as-of instant, no pattern presented as a conclusion, and no history that cannot prove itself. The fictional trial AYU-008 and its constituents are invented for this repository, and their provenance says so wherever they appear.

This is not production software. It is not clinically validated, not approved by any regulatory authority, and not affiliated with or endorsed by any institution it references for context. Its non-goals are deliberate: no live EDC integration, no ABDM exchange, no external submission receipts, no licensed dictionaries, no participant identifiers of any kind. Where a capability is planned rather than built, the interface says so — planned features are never faked.

High-level architecture

  • FastAPI + Jinja — server-rendered pages; no client framework, no hidden state.
  • SQLite · Postgres — one data layer, both drivers, boundary documented.
  • append-only audit — hash-linked events; UPDATE/DELETE refused by the database.
  • PRR signal screening — a named threshold and case floor, derived on every read, never cached.
  • escalation state machine — raise → structured response → review → leadership decision, audited at every transition.
  • session auth · role gates · CSRF — every page requires a session; every write requires a permitted role.
08 — FAQ & boundaries

Asked plainly, answered plainly.

Is this production clinical software?

No. It is a synthetic-data demonstration. It is not clinically validated, not approved by any regulatory authority, and not intended for patient care. The engineering it demonstrates — session authentication, role-gated writes, CSRF protection, append-only audit — is real; the data and the deployment context are not.

Is the data real?

No. Every trial, participant code, adverse event and audit entry is generated by a seeded fixture anchored to 2026-08-22. The schema contains no field that could hold a real participant's name, date of birth or resolvable identifier — minimization enforced by design, not by policy.

Does it replace medical judgement?

No. The system detects, organizes and retains; humans decide. Signal screening produces a triage order with its criterion visible, an escalation carries its evidence and its AI finding's review state openly, and every decision record requires a named human's rationale. Nothing is auto-closed and no conclusion is automated.

Which dictionaries are used for coding?

A curated vocabulary written for this demonstration — not MedDRA and not WHODrug, which are commercial, subscription-licensed dictionaries. The intake coder tolerates misspellings, classical terminology and cross-site synonyms, and every coded term is labelled with its vocabulary source.

How are permissions enforced?

By the server, on every request. Every page and API route requires a signed-in session; every write additionally requires a role permitted to perform that action. Interface controls merely reflect those rules — a hidden button is never the security boundary, and a link never leads to a screen the reader would be refused.

What does audit-chain verification guarantee — and not guarantee?

It guarantees the recorded history has not been altered after the fact: entries are append-only and hash-linked, the database refuses UPDATE and DELETE on the chain, and verification names the first altered row. It cannot guarantee an entry was truthful when written, and it does not prove any external authority received a filing — the system records no external receipts.

Which integrations are demonstrated?

None, live — and that is a stated boundary, not a hidden gap. There is no EDC feed, no ABDM exchange, no regulatory gateway and no FHIR/SDTM export surface in this build. What is demonstrated end-to-end is the database boundary: the same application runs on SQLite and Postgres, with an identical audit-chain head hash on both.

What should users do when data is incomplete, stale, conflicting, or urgent?

Incomplete data renders as unknown, never as zero. Stale data is labelled with when it was last known good. Conflicting states are shown with both values and their sources. And anything genuinely urgent is a filled, unmissable badge — urgency is spent only where action is actually required. In the real world, an urgent safety event follows your institution's escalation procedure, not a demo's queue.
